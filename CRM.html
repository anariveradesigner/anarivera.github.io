Overview

Exploring how AI can make onboarding more inclusive

This master's thesis explores how personalized onboarding experiences can improve accessibility and reduce cognitive load for first-time users of digital products. Rather than proposing a one-size-fits-all onboarding flow, the project investigates how onboarding can adapt to different user profiles based on their technological experience and contextual needs.

The outcome is a conceptual UX proposal for an EdTech application, where AI is integrated as a design mechanism to personalize onboarding pathways while maintaining an inclusive and accessible user experience.

Problem

Because not every user starts from the same place

Many digital products still rely on generic onboarding experiences that assume all users have similar levels of digital literacy. As a result, inexperienced users often feel overwhelmed, while more experienced users encounter unnecessary explanations that slow them down.

This challenge is reinforced by the widespread belief that well-designed products should not require onboarding at all. However, research suggests that effective onboarding increases user retention, improves confidence, and reduces abandonment, highlighting the need for more adaptive approaches that accommodate diverse user needs.

Approach

Inclusive Design & Double Diamond

This project followed an Inclusive Design approach, using human diversity as the starting point for designing more accessible onboarding experiences.

Instead of assuming that all users should interact with the same interface in the same way, the project explored how onboarding could adapt to different levels of digital literacy while maintaining a shared, accessible experience.

How it works

Helping users from the very first interaction

The experience begins with an AI assistant that asks a few questions to understand each learner's background and goals.
Based on their responses, the onboarding adapts the level of guidance and interface support. Beyond onboarding, the assistant remains available to explain lessons and activities, providing contextual help without requiring students to leave the platform.

Learnings

Design takeaways

Designing for inclusion means adapting experiences to people rather than expecting people to adapt to technology:

  • Inclusive Design starts with understanding diverse user needs, not designing for an "average" user.
  • Personalization can reduce cognitive load and improve first-time user experiences.
  • AI can enhance accessibility when used as a supportive tool, not as the product itself.
  • Early and repeated user testing led to more informed design decisions.
  • Iteration is an essential part of building meaningful user experiences.
What's next

Beyond the prototype

My next goal is to work on its launch as a fully functional prototype powered by real AI models capable of dynamically adapting onboarding based on user behavior.

Additional opportunities include validating the framework with larger and more diverse user groups, integrating behavioral analytics, measuring long-term retention, and exploring adaptive onboarding in domains beyond EdTech, such as healthcare, finance, and government digital services.
